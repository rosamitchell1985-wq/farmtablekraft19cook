The business, not the brochure

About Farm Table Kraft

Farm Table Kraft LLC is a privately owned restaurant company registered in North Carolina, operating three restaurants, one catering kitchen and a small retail line. This page explains how it makes money and how it treats the people who work in it.

What we actually are

We are a single-state restaurant company with no outside investors, no franchise arrangements and no plans to become either. Ownership is split between Marisol Vandergriff and Desmond Achebe, with a 12 percent employee ownership pool that vests after three years of service.

The company employs 118 people across the three restaurants and the catering kitchen. Roughly two thirds are full time. Everyone above 20 hours a week gets health coverage, and we publish our starting wage rather than making applicants ask for it.

We are not a chain and we are not trying to become one. Three restaurants is roughly the number that one chef can genuinely stay across, and that limit is deliberate.

Six revenue lines, and what each one is for

A restaurant that only fills a dining room has one bad winter and closes. Here is how the money actually comes in, and what we charge for each.

1. Dine-in service

The core of the business, roughly 58 percent of revenue. Three reservation tiers: free standard tables, the $25 Garden Table deposit credited to your check, and the $145 Chef's Table tasting.

Reservation tiers

2. Takeout and delivery

About 16 percent. First-party orders are at menu price with no service fee. Our own delivery is a flat $4.95 inside five miles. Third-party apps carry their own markup, which we do not set.

How ordering works

3. Catering and private events

About 15 percent, and the most seasonal line. Drop-off from $28 per guest, staffed events $65 to $110 per guest, private dining room from a $500 room fee.

Package pricing

4. Gift cards

About 6 percent, heavily weighted to November and December. Cards run $25 to $500, never expire, and carry no dormancy or inactivity fee of any kind.

Buy or check a card

5. The Twelve Mile Club

Free to join and not a revenue line in itself, but it is why about a third of our covers are repeat guests. One point per dollar on food and drink, 250 points becomes a $25 credit. No tiers, no expiry, no app to install.

Join at any host stand

6. Pantry retail

About 5 percent. Chili crisp, sorghum vinegar, house coffee and a canvas tote, priced at what they cost to make properly plus a normal retail margin.

How we pay people

Front of house starts at $18 per hour before tips. Kitchen starts at $21 per hour. Both are above the applicable state minimum in all three states we operate in, and we do not use a tip credit against wages.

In 2025 we removed the 3 percent kitchen appreciation fee that used to appear at the bottom of every check and raised menu prices to cover it instead. The total you pay did not change much. What changed is that you can now see the real price before you order, which is how it should have been from the start.

  • No automatic service charge on any check
  • No card processing surcharge passed to guests
  • Tips are pooled and distributed to the whole shift, kitchen included
  • Health coverage from 20 hours a week

What we will not do

Being clear about the limits saves everyone time.

  • We will not print a dish on the menu that we cannot source locally in that season
  • We will not add a fee to a check that was not visible before you ordered
  • We will not run fake countdowns, fake scarcity or invented review quotes on this website
  • We will not sell or share guest personal information with data brokers, and you can see exactly what we do collect in the Privacy Policy
  • We will not open a fourth restaurant while three still need this much attention
