A kitchen that answers to the growing season, not a corporate spec sheet

Farm Table Kraft started in a 38-seat storefront on Biltmore Avenue with one rule: if a grower inside our radius can produce it, we do not buy it from a distributor. That rule has survived three restaurants, two recessions and one very difficult tomato year.

Chef Marisol Vandergriff writes the core menu, then leaves twelve slots open every week. Those slots go to whatever the Blue Ridge and Lowcountry farms actually harvest. When the sweet potatoes come in heavy, you get sweet potatoes four ways. When a late frost takes the squash, we say so on the chalkboard instead of shipping something in.

  • Whole-animal butchery on site, so nothing gets thrown out
  • Bread, stock, pickles and cultured butter made in house daily
  • Every allergen tagged on the printed and online menu

What twelve miles actually buys you

A tomato picked at dawn twelve miles from the pass reaches the plate before it ever needs refrigeration. That is the whole argument. It is not a marketing radius. It is a driving distance our produce buyer covers three mornings a week in a 2016 Ford Transit.

Nineteen growers supply us under seasonal agreements written a year ahead, so a farm knows what we will buy before they plant it. We pay within seven days of delivery rather than the thirty that is standard in this industry.
