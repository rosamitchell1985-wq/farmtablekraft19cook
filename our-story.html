Eleven years in

Our story, and the chef who writes the menu

Farm Table Kraft began with a lease we could barely afford and one rule we refused to bend. That rule is still the reason the menu changes every Tuesday, and the reason some weeks are harder than others.

Meet Marisol Vandergriff

Marisol grew up on the edge of Madison County, North Carolina, in a house where the freezer was full every October and empty by April. She cooked in Charleston and then Chicago for nine years, came back in 2013, and spent a full season driving between farms before she wrote a single dish.

Her argument is simple and she has not changed it in eleven years: a cook who knows the grower can cook better than a cook who knows the invoice. If you know that a farm's carrots came out of heavy clay this year, you cook them longer. If you know a hog was pastured on acorns, you salt the belly differently. None of that survives a distribution chain.

She is in the Asheville kitchen four days a week, Charleston one, and Nashville one, and she still writes every menu by hand on kraft stock before it is typeset.

If I cannot tell you the name of the person who grew it, I should not be charging you thirty-four dollars for it.

Marisol Vandergriff, executive chef and co-owner

Eleven years, told straight

No mythology. These are the years that actually shaped how the company runs today, including the two we would rather skip.

Company timeline
YearWhat happened
2014Farm Table Kraft opens on Biltmore Avenue with 38 seats, six staff and a chalkboard because we could not afford printed menus.
2015Whole-animal butchery starts in the basement after a hog farmer offers a better price for taking the entire animal.
2016Sourdough programme begins. The starter from that autumn is still raising every loaf in all three restaurants.
2017We raise the dining room to 78 seats by taking over the empty unit next door, and start paying growers in seven days instead of thirty.
2019Charleston opens on King Street. The first year is genuinely difficult and we come close to closing it.
2020Both restaurants close to dining for eleven weeks. We turn the Asheville kitchen into a produce box operation and keep 31 of 44 staff employed through it.
2021Twelve Mile Club rewards launches, built deliberately without tiers, expiry dates or a mobile app you have to install.
2022Seasonal grower agreements move to twelve-month contracts, so farms know what we will buy before they plant it.
2023Nashville opens in Germantown. First location built with an accessible entrance designed in rather than retrofitted.
2024Chef's Table tasting starts in Asheville. Sells out for fourteen consecutive weeks, then settles into a normal rhythm.
2025Kitchen appreciation fee is scrapped and rolled into menu prices, because a surprise line on a check is not a wage policy.

What the twelve mile rule costs us

Being honest about the downside is part of the deal. The rule means we pay more per pound than a distributor would charge, roughly 18 to 24 percent more on produce. It means we cannot promise you a specific dish next Thursday. It means a hailstorm in Henderson County shows up on our menu two days later.

What it buys is a kitchen that can taste the difference and a farm that can plan a season. We think that trade is worth it, and we would rather explain it than quietly stop doing it.
