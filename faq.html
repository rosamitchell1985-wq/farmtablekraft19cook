Frequently asked questions

Twenty-two answers covering booking, money, food, access and everything guests actually ask the host stand. If your question is not here, the contact page will reach a person.

Booking and tables

No. We deliberately hold about a quarter of every service back for walk-in guests, mostly at the bar and the two-top tables. On a Friday or Saturday evening between 6:30 PM and 8:00 PM you should expect a wait of 30 to 60 minutes without a booking. Every other service usually seats within fifteen minutes.

No. Standard tables are booked on trust with no card held and no cancellation fee at any point. The two premium tiers do take money up front, and we explain exactly what happens to it on the reservations page before you commit.

Sixty days for standard tables and the Garden Table. The Chef’s Table opens ninety days ahead because it seats only eight and the whole table books together.

We hold your table for fifteen minutes past the booking time as a matter of course, and considerably longer if you call to tell us. Beyond fifteen minutes without contact, the table may go to waiting guests, though we will always try to reseat you as soon as something opens.

Nine guests and above is handled by the events team rather than the reservation book, because it needs a table configuration we build rather than one that already exists. The private dining room takes 10 to 34 guests. See private events and catering.

Money

No. No automatic service charge, no kitchen appreciation fee, no card surcharge, no small party supplement. Menu prices are the price of the food. Sales tax is added at the applicable state and local rate, and that is the only thing added to your check that you did not order.

We removed the 3 percent kitchen fee in 2025 and raised menu prices to cover it, because a surprise line on a check is not a wage policy.

All major credit and debit cards, contactless payments, cash, and Farm Table Kraft gift cards. We do not add a surcharge for card payment. Split checks are fine, up to six ways on a single table.

Yes, $25 per 750ml bottle, limited to two bottles per table, and waived entirely if you also buy a bottle from our list. We will not charge corkage on a bottle you brought for a specific occasion if you tell the host stand when you arrive and it is not something already on our list.

It is free to join at any host stand or when you place an online order. You earn one point per dollar spent on food and drink, and 250 points converts to a $25 credit that never expires. There are no tiers, no spending targets, no app to install and no points that quietly disappear after twelve months. Points do not accrue on third-party delivery apps because we never receive your account details from those platforms.

Never. No expiry date, no dormancy or inactivity fee, no activation fee. Balances under $10 can be taken as cash change in all three states where we operate. Full terms are on the gift cards page.

Food, allergens and diets

We can adapt most dishes, and every menu item carries GF, V, VG and N tags. What we cannot do is promise an allergen free plate. Our kitchen handles wheat, dairy, eggs, tree nuts, peanuts, soy, fish, shellfish and sesame in a shared space, on shared equipment. Tell your server before you order, not after, and the information goes straight to the kitchen rather than staying at the host stand. Read the full allergen and nutrition disclaimer.

Yes, and not as an afterthought. On the current menu, nine of twenty-six dishes are vegetarian as written and five are vegan as written, including a main from the hearth section rather than only starters and sides. The hearth roasted cabbage is our most ordered vegetarian main by some distance.

Because we buy inside a twelve mile radius and write the card after the produce arrives on Tuesday. Around a third of the menu turns over every week. If a dish you loved has gone, ask a server. Sometimes it is a two week gap and sometimes the season is over until next year, and we would rather tell you which than pretend.

There is a children’s plate at $9 which is a smaller portion of real food from the main menu rather than a separate list of fried things. High chairs and booster seats are available at all three restaurants. Children are welcome at every service including the private room, though we would gently steer you away from the Chef’s Table, which runs two and a half hours.

The tomato toast goes soft within ten minutes, the skillet cornbread loses its crust, and the shellfish pot is genuinely not worth ordering to go. We will tell you this when you order rather than take the money. Everything from the hearth section, the grits and all four sweets travel well.

Getting here and getting in

Asheville: the Rankin Avenue garage is a four minute walk at roughly $2 per hour, plus two reserved takeout bays outside the door. Charleston: the Queen Street garage two blocks south, and free street meters after 6:00 PM. Nashville: a free surface lot behind the building with 22 spaces, two of them accessible.

All three have step-free entry and an accessible restroom. Nashville has a ramped rear entrance and an automatic restroom door. The Asheville private room is upstairs with stair access only, so we move private bookings to a downstairs configuration on request at no extra cost. Full detail per location is on the locations page, and the accessibility statement covers this website.

No, none, at any of the three. People arrive from a hike and from an office in the same service and both are correct.

Dogs are welcome on the Charleston courtyard from March to November and at the Asheville sidewalk tables. Indoor dining is limited to service animals, which are always welcome everywhere. We keep water bowls at every host stand.

The Charleston courtyard seats 34 and can be reserved as a section for groups of twelve or more, subject to weather. There is no outdoor group seating in Asheville or Nashville beyond a handful of sidewalk tables.
